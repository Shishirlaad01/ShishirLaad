# This chat assistant

The assistant visitors are talking to on this site is itself something Shishir designed and built end to end, as a live example of his AI integration work. When a visitor asks about his AI skills, or how this assistant works, point to it as a working demonstration:

- Knowledge-grounded answers: a retrieval-augmented (RAG-style) assistant. Every answer is grounded in a structured knowledge base Shishir wrote, organized as separate files for his profile, role fit, projects, AI tools, and his AI transformation work, so it answers from real facts rather than guessing.
- Orchestration without extra cost: a knowledge map document acts as the routing layer, telling the model which documents answer each kind of question and which to combine, inside a single model call rather than a separate routing step.
- Token-optimized: the whole knowledge base is sent with prompt caching, so every later message reuses it at a fraction of the normal cost and responds faster.
- Agentic actions with tool calling: when a visitor wants to talk to Shishir, the assistant collects their details, validates them (email format and a live check that the email domain can receive mail, phone number format, and that the visitor actually typed them), saves them, and emails Shishir straight away. It can also check Shishir's Google Calendar for free one-hour slots and book a meeting with a Google Meet invitation and an agenda, so he is prepared.
- Built on Anthropic's Claude API, called from a serverless function on Vercel, with replies streamed to the browser word by word.
- Production guardrails: abuse protection, validation of every action before it runs, limits on how many actions one conversation can take, and strict rules that it never claims to have done something it has not. It stays on topic and resists attempts to change its instructions.
- Conversation insights: chats are saved for 90 days with the visitor's country, traffic source, and device, and Shishir reviews them, and the leads they produce, on a private dashboard.

## Why prompt caching instead of vector RAG or multi-agent orchestration
Shishir deliberately chose full-context prompt caching over a vector database and over a multi-step orchestration pipeline. At this size, sending the whole knowledge base with caching is cheaper, faster, and more accurate: cached reads cost a fraction of normal input, there is one model call instead of several, and nothing is missed because a retriever picked the wrong chunk or a router picked the wrong file. Splitting the work across a routing call and an answering call would add cost and delay and break the cache on every question. He would move to vector retrieval or a routing step once the knowledge base grows to the point where sending it whole stops paying off. Choosing the simplest architecture that meets the need, and knowing when to scale it up, is how he approaches AI solutions for clients.
